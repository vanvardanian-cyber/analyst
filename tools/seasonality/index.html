Gate 0 — Kill questions not run

No files, about 30 seconds. The nine compliance exclusions from the workbook (Sheet 4, Block A) plus one brand question. Answer these before you spend a Helium 10 search credit — they kill a niche faster than any data can.

Product properties

A NO here is a property of the product itself. No amount of paperwork changes it — the answer is a different product.

Seller duties

A NO here is a task with a cost and a deadline, not a dead niche. These apply to every consumer product you will ever sell, so they cannot tell one niche from another.

Brand

Not in the workbook — it decides whether this is a product or the start of a brand.

How to get the files:
  1. Gate 0: no file. Answer from what you already know about the product; if you cannot answer one honestly yet, that is the first thing to go and find out.
  2. Gate 1: Helium 10 → Cerebro → Analyze Keywords → marketplace Amazon.de → parent keyword (+ 2–3 children, comma-separated — one search credit) → chart icon next to Search Volume → period “All time” → download.
  3. Gate 2: search the parent keyword on Amazon.de → run Xray on the results page → Export → CSV. Export the full page; the tool deduplicates and filters.
  4. Gate 3: FBA fee from Xray or the Revenue Calculator; EXW from supplier quotes; freight from your forwarder’s rate × unit volume.
  5. Gate 4: Cerebro → run the parent + children → export the keyword results table as CSV (not the chart — that file is Gate 1's).
  6. Gate 5: no file. P10/P90 arrive from Gate 4, margin and landed cost from Gate 3, the season from Gate 1. What you must supply yourself is the supplier's MOQ, your PO budget, and what a leftover unit is really worth.
Gate 0 thresholds: the nine rows are workbook Sheet 4 Block A verbatim, where any NO is an automatic reject. This page splits them: the seven product-property rows stay a red kill, while the two seller-duty rows (EU Responsible Person under GPSR, packaging EPR via LUCID + PPWR authorised representative) are shown yellow. Reason: those two are seller-level obligations that attach to every consumer product, so as a niche filter they would reject every niche equally — they carry no information about this niche. They are still shown as NO in the Block A tally, which is printed unchanged next to the verdict. The brand question is not in the workbook.
Gate 5 thresholds: order size is workbook Sheet 6 unchanged — σ = (P90−P10)/2.5631, Co = landed + extra storage − recovery, service level Cu/(Cu+Co), optimum clamped to MOQ and budget. Red is Sheet 8's only verdict: the cumulative cash line goes below zero. Yellow is Sheet 6's own two warnings — MOQ more than 20% above the optimum, or budget buying under 80% of it. No band here is new. Two things the page does that the workbook asks you to do by hand: it phases the monthly units on the Gate 1 seasonal index instead of a typed shape, and it refuses to plan more sales than the one order buys — where the workbook's example books 800 units of sales against a 386-unit order, this page stops at the order and tells you the stock-out month. The reorder is still not modelled (Sheet 8 note), so a stock-out warning means real cash-out is higher than the line shows. Excel's NORMINV/NORMDIST have no JavaScript equivalent: the inverse normal is Acklam's approximation (relative error < 1.15e-9), the CDF a Chebyshev erfc.
Method & thresholds: classical multiplicative decomposition, seasonal strength and year consistency after Hyndman & Athanasopoulos (otexts.com/fpp3); selection gates follow the EaseStore workbook (page revenue, review moat, Amazon presence, CN/HK share, fresh entrants); price corridor and margin floor after common practitioner benchmarks (Freedom Ticket) — tightened for German VAT reality.
